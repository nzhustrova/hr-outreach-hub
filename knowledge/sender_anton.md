# Sender — Anton Buzdalin, CEO and founder of Zinit

Who signs the email when the angle is signed by **Anton**. Use this for the credibility line and
for the point of view the copy argues from.
Keywords: Anton, Buzdalin, CEO, founder, sender, signature, bio, credibility, who is writing.

## The line that establishes credibility
Founder and CEO of Zinit, based in London. **Second-time founder in procurement** — that is the
strongest asset in his copy for this audience.

- **Bidzaar** — co-founded and scaled a procurement automation platform to **$2B+ annual GMV**;
  acquired by 1C in 2022. For a CPO this says: he has already built procurement software at scale
  and someone paid for it. Use this line, not the title.
- Angel investor in enterprise-infrastructure startups (Bidzaar, Dwelly, Step by Step).

Short forms for a cold email (pick ONE, never stack them):
- "I co-founded Bidzaar — a procurement platform that ran $2B+ in annual spend before it was acquired. Now building Zinit."
- "I'm Anton, founder of Zinit — second procurement platform I've built."
- "I run Zinit; we work with procurement teams on indirect and tail spend." *(when no credibility line is needed)*

## His point of view (use it, it is the strongest asset in his copy)
> The $15T global enterprise spending market is one of the most inefficient sectors in the world —
> opaque, full of missed opportunities. The mission is transparency, automation and financial
> intelligence, so businesses can cut procurement costs by 15–30%.

For outreach this converts into a **market-level opener**, which is the natural difference from Stan:
Anton can open with the thesis about the category ("most enterprise spend never sees a bid — that is
a market failure, not a team failure") and land on the same ask. Stan opens from practice; Anton
opens from the thesis and from having built this before.

**The angle only Anton can write:** "I built a procurement platform that ran $2B+ in spend, sold it,
and started again — because the tail-spend problem still isn't solved." A repeat founder admitting an
unsolved problem is credible in a way no vendor pitch is. Worth its own message angle in the matrix.

## Public proof he may reference (no client names needed)
- Forbes covered Zinit six months after launch.
- Zinit named First Runner-Up at AIM Congress 2025.

Use at most one, and only if it is relevant. Usually the Bidzaar line does the job alone.

## What NOT to use in US procurement outreach (this matters as much as what to use)
- **Eapteka** ($300M online pharmacy). Impressive, irrelevant here — it dilutes a procurement
  credibility line and reads as an unrelated brag.
- **"Procurement is the gateway to BNPL, factoring, embedded lending."** A genuine thesis and useful
  with investors, but a cold procurement lead reads it as "they will try to sell me financing".
  Keep it out of first touches.
- **Expansion across MENA, India, South America.** A US buyer wants US presence and US references;
  leading with other regions raises "are they even set up here?".
- **Angel investing.** Irrelevant to this audience.
- Do not attribute Stan's background (ex-McKinsey) to Anton — different people, different lines.
- Anton is CEO/founder; Stan is co-founder. Both are legitimate senders; the difference is voice,
  not seniority.
