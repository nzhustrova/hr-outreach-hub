# Play — discovery questions that work, and the KPI set

Pulled from deals that advanced. The first four qualify or kill a deal inside ten minutes.
Useful for reply drafting: a good reply often ends with one of these, not with a pitch.
Keywords: discovery, qualifying questions, call, KPI, success criteria, pilot.

## The four that qualify or kill
1. **What is your competitive-bid threshold, and how often does it get waived?**
   When a buyer answers this honestly, they state your business case for you.
2. **What tool do your buyers use for RFPs today?**
   "Excel and email" is the green light. A live source-to-pay rollout is a red light.
3. **Which indirect vendors are already under contract?**
   Determines whether the GPO wedge exists at all.
4. **Who owns indirect procurement, and are you aligned with them?**
   More deals have stalled on this gap than on price. Ask it on the first call, not the fourth.

## The next four
- How much of your spend has line-item detail versus free text? *(Determines whether GPO matching is possible.)*
- How much runs on corporate cards with no visibility? *(Opens the Maverick agent.)*
- What does your team do when a plant says only one local supplier exists? *(Opens supplier discovery.)*
- What would you need to see in a pilot to justify expanding it? *(Gets the KPI list before you propose.)*

## Default KPI set when a prospect asks what success looks like
- Savings as pre- versus post-price delta × volume.
- Number of new suppliers engaged versus incumbents per event.
- Percentage of events won by non-incumbents.
- Cycle time from RFP creation to first bid, and to award.
- Percentage of $10K–$250K spend addressed through structured sourcing.

## Expectation to set up front
New-supplier award rate in year one will sit below 30%, because supplier validation takes time.
Say it early rather than miss it later.
