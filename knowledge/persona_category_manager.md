# Persona — category / indirect procurement manager (where the pain is real)

Titles: Category Manager, Indirect Procurement Manager, Sourcing Manager, Procurement Manager,
Purchasing Manager, Head of Indirect. This is where Zinit wins and where the pain is immediate.
Keywords: category manager, indirect, buyer, RFQ, manual, plants, workload, bandwidth.

## What hurts
- Manual RFQs by email; two-week alignment cycles for a single RFP.
- No supplier alternatives; plants insist only one local supplier exists.
- Blanket POs with no line-item record.
- More scope than hours. The team is the bottleneck, not the savings idea.

## What sells
The **live demo**. Drop a spreadsheet, watch an RFP appear. This persona judges the product in the
first ninety seconds of seeing it. Speed and effort-removal, not strategy.

## What kills it
Anything that adds work. Buyers have passed on double-digit savings purely because the team had no
bandwidth to action them. **Savings alone do not move this person — effort-to-capture does.**

## Framing rules
- ❌ Never use "do more with less" / headcount-efficiency framing with this persona — they are the
  buyer and hear it as a threat to their own team. That framing is for CFO and investor conversations.
- ✅ Lead with time and workload relief: "bid the spend nobody has hours to bid".
- ✅ Concede honestly that supplier engagement still needs people — the supplier success team absorbs
  most of it. Overclaiming autonomy to a sophisticated buyer costs more than it gains.

## Best-fit outreach angle
Hiring-signal angle (A3) and the pain-question angle (A2) both land: both are about workload, not savings.
Follow-up value step: the GPO benchmark — it asks for almost nothing from them (see `play_gpo_wedge`).
