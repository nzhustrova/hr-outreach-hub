# Pricing — the three commercial models and the fee questions

Answer for "what does it cost?", "how do you make money?", "will suppliers just raise prices?".
Roughly 80% of clients take the supplier-funded model.
Keywords: pricing, cost, fee, subscription, supplier-funded, setup fee, commission, budget.

## ⚠️ OPEN CONTRADICTION with the investor deck (ТЗ §8 п.13) — do not resolve it yourself
This card (from the GTM playbook): supplier-funded **0.1–1%**, cap $15K per event, **setup fee $30K**
(often waived). The investor deck v4 says: **$0 annual fee**, "no upfront software investment",
success fee **0.01–1%**, "paid on wins only". The percentage differs 10× and the deck denies the very
setup fee this card describes. An investor deck is not a client offer, so the two must NOT be silently
merged. **Until Stan confirms what we tell leads about money: describe only the SHAPE (see
"Cold-outreach guidance" below), never quote a percentage, a cap or the setup fee in writing.**
*(Marked 2026-08-13, question is with Stan.)*

| Model | Structure | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Supplier-funded** (default) | Winning supplier pays 0.1–1% of awarded value, capped at $15K per event, charged only at award — not per PO. Client pays a one-time setup fee, waived above an agreed commitment. | Default. Removes the budget fight for a category manager with no software budget. |
| **Performance / success fee** | A percentage of verified savings, methodology agreed up front. | When the buyer wants risk shared and has a savings-tracking culture. |
| **Straight subscription** | Roughly $100–120K a year all in, no variable fees. | When supplier fees are politically impossible, or the client refuses to have their suppliers charged. |

## The setup fee
$30K one-time is the standard number, frequently waived or negotiated. Where the fee is the blocker,
a reduced fee or a performance-tied structure has been used.
⚠️ **Do not improvise the waiver threshold** — it has been quoted several different ways within a single
quarter. Confirm the current threshold with Stan before stating it in writing. Where a spend floor has
proved unreachable, replacing it with a committed number of sourcing events works better — event count
is something a procurement lead can actually forecast.

## "Will suppliers just add 1% to their price?" (asked every time)
> Historically suppliers pass along about half the fee — five basis points to half a percent. Against
> that, new competitive pressure produces a median above 15%, and 67% of awards still go to incumbents
> at better prices than before.

Expect a sharper version from anyone who has lived through a large supplier-network rollout at a legacy
vendor. Those buyers have seen supplier friction firsthand and often prefer a client-funded model if the
tool will be deployed broadly. **Do not fight it — price it.**

## Know which model the account is on
Not every enterprise client is on supplier-funded; some sign a subscription with supplier commissions
switched off. If a prospect asks who else pays under the supplier-funded model, verify the account you
name actually does before naming it.

## Cold-outreach guidance
In a first reply to "what does it cost", the useful answer is the *shape*, not a quote:
no subscription in the default model, the winning supplier pays a fraction of a percent at award, and a
subscription option exists if they prefer their suppliers untouched. Exact numbers and the setup fee
belong in a call with Stan, not in an outbound email thread.
