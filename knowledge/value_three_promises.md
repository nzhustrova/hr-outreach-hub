# Value — the three promises, in the order that works

The pitch is built on time reduction, savings and efficiency. This replaced an earlier framing built
on digitization and transparency, which did not land. Use the current one.
Keywords: value proposition, promise, coverage, cycle time, savings, tail spend, unbid spend.

⚠️ **US framing, corrected 2026-08-11 by Stan:** promise 1 below is written tail-first. On the US market
the promise is **Spend Under Management** — the share of spend that goes through competitive sourcing.
The tail is the bottom row of that table, not the headline. See `product_positioning_us`.

## Promise 1 — coverage: raise Spend Under Management
The strongest opening, because it moves a number the buyer already reports upwards and is already
measured on — not a new metric we have to sell them on first.

Coverage today against coverage with Zinit (investor deck 11.08): above $1M — 40–50% → 100%; between
$250K and $1M — 10–30% → 80–100%; below $250K — practically nothing → about 80%.

The bottom band goes unbid because the effort exceeds the perceived reward. Zinit changes that
arithmetic — but it is **one third of the story, not the story**. Leading with it caps the conversation
at a fraction of their spend (see `product_positioning_us`).

## Promise 2 — cycle time
Updated to the deck figures (11.08): the **full RFP cycle** goes from 3–6 months to 2–4 weeks — an 83%
reduction. Inside it: RFP preparation 2–4 weeks → about an hour; supplier discovery 1–2 weeks → under
three minutes; negotiations 2–4 weeks → 1–3 days. First RFP live in under an hour, with no integration.

## Promise 3 — savings, with the mechanism explained
**12–18%** on neglected indirect and tail categories, **15%+ median** across the client base.
The mechanism matters more than the number, because the number invites disbelief:

> 67% of awards still go to incumbent suppliers, at better prices. You are not asking anyone to switch
> vendors. You are introducing competitive pressure to a category that has never had any.

## Buyer value proposition (full list, for longer-form copy)
- **Time and labour** — removes costly manual sourcing work; US procurement labour is expensive.
- **Do more with less** — headcount efficiency. ⚠️ CFO/investor framing only; never to procurement staff.
- **Levels up the team** — standardizes execution so quality does not depend on who is doing the task.
- **Solves the real bottleneck: supplier verification** — US enterprise buyers do not lack tenders,
  they lack capacity to vet suppliers at scale.
- **Document vault** — procurement documents, templates and SOPs in one place; agents apply the rules
  instead of a buyer checking hundreds of pages of policy.
- **Data security** — procurement data stays inside the platform, not fed to general-purpose LLMs.
- **Compounding moat** — a verified, growing supplier database is expensive for competitors to copy.
- **Net result:** control more spend, cut cost, keep compliance and security intact.

## Supplier value proposition (when the lead is a supplier, not a buyer)
One onboarding, unlimited access: kill the onboarding paperwork repeated for every buyer; a new sales
channel into enterprise buyers; no more one-off capability decks; same qualification effort applies
across many sourcing events.
