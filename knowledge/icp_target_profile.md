# ICP — who to target: revenue, industry, procurement structure, spend

Ideal customer profile for Zinit outreach. Built backwards from what closed, stalled and died.
Keywords: ICP, target, segment, manufacturing, industrials, revenue, indirect spend, tail spend,
decentralized procurement, Excel and email, competitive bid threshold.

## Tier 1 — where deals actually close
Every signed US contract to date fits this shape.

| Signal | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $1B–$50B. Large enough to have real indirect spend, small enough that procurement is not fully staffed. |
| Industry | Manufacturing, automotive, industrials, distribution. Physical sites, physical goods, plant-level buying. |
| Procurement structure | Indirect is decentralized or barely staffed. Divisions or plants buy on their own. No central category ownership. |
| Current tooling | Excel and email. No e-sourcing tool, or one that failed adoption. **Strongest signal:** a large multi-entity group with no RFP tool anywhere in it. |
| Team size | Central team of ~5–15 people covering far more scope than they can serve. |
| Addressable spend | $10M+ in MRO, facilities, uniforms, office supplies, packaging, small parcel. |

**The tell.** Someone says out loud that events below a threshold never get bid. Typical answer:
a $250K policy threshold, routinely waived, with ~100 events a year that should have gone
competitive and did not. A failed legacy e-sourcing deployment is our best qualified lead.

## Tier 2 — real, but slower and structurally different
- **PE firms and portfolio companies.** Want the supplier-discovery database standalone, not the suite.
  Refuse supplier-funded pricing in favour of direct licensing. Buying trigger is exit-readiness.
- **Consulting firms as a channel.** Position as a transactional cycle-time accelerator, never a
  strategic platform; sometimes white-labelled. Way in: one burning category on a live engagement.
  These buyers move in hours, not sales cycles.

## Disqualifiers — stop early
| Disqualifier | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| High procurement maturity, no tail spend | Spend already negotiated; process automation has no value. |
| Single-source or unique direct parts | Alternative-supplier matching is not viable (semiconductors, aerospace, regulated components). |
| Every indirect vendor already under contract | GPO value is zero — but may still be a good eRFX prospect; qualify the two products separately. |
| Low event volume (<~100/yr) + confidentiality rules | Spend density too low to justify either model. |
| Mid-rollout of a competing source-to-pay suite | No evaluation until the incumbent rollout completes. Set a reminder, offer the free GPO benchmark meanwhile. |
| Buyer is not in procurement | No procurement mandate, no purchase, however enthusiastic. |
| Freight / carrier execution is the actual need | Outside the product. Reset scope or decline. |

## How this maps to our search panel
- Industry filter → Industrial Manufacturing, Manufacturing, Automotive, Logistics & Supply Chain, Construction.
- Company size → 1001–5000 and 5000+ carry the Tier-1 shape; mid-market 201–1000 is a test, not the core.
- Revenue filter → $1B+ matches Tier 1 exactly (Advanced filters, Apollo).
- Hiring-procurement signal → proxy for "team can't cover the scope", which is the Tier-1 pain.
