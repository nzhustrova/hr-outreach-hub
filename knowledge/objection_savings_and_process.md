# Objections — savings scepticism, tail spend, GPO history, bandwidth

Keywords: savings doubt, benchmarks, list prices, tail spend consolidation, new suppliers, GPO rate
creep, no bandwidth, onboarding fee, ROI.

## "We'll create new tail suppliers by running these events."
Preferred suppliers can be tagged and events closed to the approved vendor list only. Then the counter-
fact: **67% of awards go to incumbents anyway, at better prices.** The bidding is what improves the
incumbent's price.

## "Tail spend consolidation doesn't always produce savings — low volume, high SKU count, suppliers won't engage."
Two levers: bring additional suppliers in to establish price transparency and force competition, or
consolidate the tail into large distributors already in their supply base. This objection comes from
people who have tried tail consolidation and failed — **treat it seriously rather than rebutting it**.

## "Your benchmarks are list prices, not our negotiated prices."
The GPO matching is item-level exact match against contracted catalogue prices, not an estimate.
Offer to prove it on a subset of their data in two days. **That offer is what makes the objection go away.**

## "The onboarding fee is a hard sell without proven ROI."
Options used: reduce the fee, tie it to performance, or run the free GPO benchmark first so a savings
number exists before the fee is discussed. Do not let the fee surface late in the cycle — it kills
momentum when it appears as a surprise.

## "We've been burned by GPO rate creep." ⚠️ our answer is not good enough yet
Buyers who moved off a GPO to their own contract because of pricing practices will not be reassured by
savings numbers. What we have today is the non-committal structure — the client can leave at any time,
no penalty, no lock-in. Ongoing price monitoring is the part still missing. **Do not oversell here.**

## "We don't have the bandwidth to action the savings you found."
The most common silent killer in the pipeline. Counter by **ranking opportunities by effort, not by
size**: start with same-distributor substitutions that require no buyer conversation at all, then move
to harder switches once there is a win on the board.

## Where deals stall, and what unstuck them
| Failure pattern | Counter-move that worked |
|---|---|
| Champion can't align the indirect procurement counterpart | Identify the counterpart on call one and ask to include them. Do not run a second demo for the champion alone. |
| Gated behind another system rollout | Accept it, set a reminder, offer the free GPO benchmark meanwhile — it needs no system decision. |
| Setup fee surfaces late | Put pricing on the table early; run the GPO first so a savings number exists before the fee does. |
| Spend commitment thresholds the client can't meet | Drop the spend floor; commit to a number of sourcing events instead. |
| Legal review queue of 2–4 weeks | Send a one-to-three page pilot agreement, not an MSA; begin onboarding in parallel in good faith. |
| No bandwidth to capture savings | Sequence opportunities by effort; start with switches needing no buyer conversation. |
